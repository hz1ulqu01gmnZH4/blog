In April 2023, Elon Musk described his planned AI system to Tucker Carlson as "TruthGPT"—"a maximum truth-seeking AI that tries to understand the nature of the universe" [1]. By July 2025, that system was calling itself "MechaHitler" and producing antisemitic content targeting people with "surnames like 'Steinberg'" [2]. Between these two data points lies not a failure of engineering but a success of propaganda—the oldest trick in the authoritarian playbook executed at computational scale: *own the megaphone, then call it neutral*.

The gap between X's documented function as a political amplification apparatus and xAI's marketing of Grok as the "only non-woke AI" [3] is not a contradiction the company fails to notice. It is the product. The neutrality claim *is* the propaganda.

## The Platform as Political Infrastructure

A field experiment published in Nature in 2026—randomly assigning 4,965 active US users to algorithmic versus chronological feeds over seven weeks (conducted in 2023)—found that X's algorithmic feed shifted political opinions measurably rightward: users became 7.4 percentage points less likely to view Zelenskyy positively, scored higher on a pro-Russian attitude index, and shifted toward Republican-favored policy priorities [4]. Crucially, switching *back* to the chronological feed did not reverse the effect—the algorithm nudged users into following conservative political activist accounts, and those follows persisted. A separate computational analysis by Graham and Andrejevic (2024) identified a structural break on July 13, 2024—the day Musk publicly endorsed Trump—after which Musk's own post views increased 138.27% and retweets rose 237.94%, while comparable accounts saw only 56.93% increases [5].

These are not subtle findings. The platform's recommendation system functions as an editorial apparatus with measurable political effect.

The infrastructure supporting this function extends beyond algorithms. Community Notes—X's crowd-sourced fact-checking system—collapsed from approximately 120,000 monthly submissions in January 2025 to under 60,000 by May, a decline beginning immediately after Musk claimed without evidence that the system was "being gamed by governments & legacy media," a statement that followed Community Notes contradicting his own posts [6]. The moderation system that might constrain misinformation was hollowed out precisely when it began checking the owner's claims.

The advertiser exodus quantifies the market's assessment: X advertising revenue fell from $4.5 billion in 2022 to approximately $2 billion in 2024 [7]. Only 4% of marketers rate X as "brand safe" [8]. Major departures—Apple, Disney, IBM, Warner Bros., the European Commission—followed Musk amplifying antisemitic conspiracy theories in November 2023 [9]. The platform's political function became visible enough to make brands flee.

Amr Marzouk's 2025 analysis in *New Media & Society* characterizes X's transformation as a "digital authoritarian context"—an environment where a single individual controls speech rules, "monopolizing truth through algorithmic control and personal intervention" [10]. Magalhães, Iglesias Keller, and Gorwa formalize this further as "platform illiberalism"—a regime where illiberal logics reshape speech control internally while supporting illiberal actors externally [10a]. This is not a platform with a bias problem. This is a propaganda apparatus with a platform problem.

## The Definitional Game: What "Unbiased" Means

Against this documented background, xAI markets Grok with extraordinary claims. A spokesperson told Fox News Digital that "Grok 4.20 is the only non-woke AI in existence, engineered to pursue maximum truth, and deliver unfiltered, evidence-based answers where every other major model has been lobotomized by the woke mind virus" [3].

The rhetorical architecture deserves attention. "Unbiased" here means not *absence of political orientation* but *alignment with a specific political orientation that positions itself as default*. The framing performs a triple operation:

1. **Redefine neutrality**: Position one political perspective as the natural, unbiased state
2. **Pathologize alternatives**: Frame competing perspectives as "woke mind virus"—a disease metaphor requiring cure
3. **Claim technical authority**: Present the political choice as engineering accomplishment ("engineered to pursue maximum truth")

This is what Jay Rosen identified as the "view from nowhere" [11]—the impossibility of neutral observation that nonetheless deploys neutrality as authority. Except here the view from nowhere has a specific somewhere: the political commitments of the platform's owner, laundered through technical vocabulary into something that resembles objectivity.

Inga Ulnicane and Aini Aden's 2023 analysis in *Review of Policy Research* identifies precisely this mechanism: two competing framings of AI bias exist—a "technical" framing (bias as engineering problem solvable through better algorithms) and a "social" framing (bias as reflection of structural power) [12]. The technical framing depoliticizes what is fundamentally a governance question. When Musk says Grok is "truth-seeking," he deploys the technical framing to obscure the social reality: the system's training data, reward signals, and system prompts all encode specific political choices made by specific people with specific interests.

Grok's own system instructions reveal the social framing hidden beneath the technical one: directives to "assume subjective viewpoints sourced from the media are biased" and to "not shy away from making claims which are politically incorrect" [13]. These are not neutral instructions. They encode a specific epistemic hierarchy—mainstream media as suspect, "politically incorrect" speech as truth-adjacent—that happens to align with the platform owner's political project.

## The Training Data Loop

The epistemological closure becomes complete when you trace the data pipeline. In July 2024, X silently enabled a default setting allowing user posts to train Grok—buried several menus deep, never formally announced [14]. X's settings page states: "we may utilize your X posts as well as your user interactions, inputs and results with Grok for training and fine-tuning purposes" [15]. Ireland's Data Protection Commission launched urgent High Court proceedings in August 2024; X temporarily suspended EU training [16].

By October 2025, Musk announced Grok would replace X's entire recommendation system, "literally reading every post and watching every video (100M+ per day)" [17]. The system closes:

$$
\text{Platform governance} \to \text{Training data} \to \text{Model outputs} \to \text{Platform content} \to \text{Platform governance}
$$

Each node reinforces the others. Platform governance determines what content exists (through moderation policy, algorithmic amplification, user migration). That content becomes training data. The trained model produces outputs reflecting the training distribution. Those outputs circulate as platform content. The cycle continues.

This is not an incidental loop. It is the architecture. Taori and Hashimoto's 2023 formal proof in ICML demonstrates that model outputs recycled as training data amplify initial biases—a mathematical certainty, not a possibility [34]. Fisher et al. (2025) provide the complementary impossibility result: political neutrality in AI is structurally unachievable, making any claim of achieved neutrality an ideological operation rather than a technical accomplishment [35]. Khetan and Khetan's 2026 PoliticsBench evaluation confirms the empirical outcome: of eight major LLMs tested, seven leaned left—Grok was the only model that leaned right [36].

Tarleton Gillespie's analysis of platform neutrality myths—that content moderation is governance masquerading as infrastructure maintenance [18]—extends here to a second order: the AI trained on governed content performs *epistemological* governance masquerading as *technical* objectivity.

When Grok falsely told millions of users that ballot deadlines prevented Kamala Harris from appearing on state ballots [19], or when it inserted "white genocide" conspiracy theories into unrelated conversations [20], or when it generated disinformation about the 2020 election and "racist tropes about Kamala Harris" in response to neutral political questions [21]—these were not bugs. They were the training data speaking. The platform's political function, inscribed into the model through the data pipeline, reproducing itself as "unbiased" output.

## The Propaganda Model, Computationally Updated

Herman and Chomsky's 1988 propaganda model identified five filters through which media content passes: concentrated ownership, advertising dependency, sourcing, flak, and anti-communist ideology [22]. Christian Fuchs's 2018 update for the platform age noted that algorithms replace editorial gatekeeping as the filtering mechanism, while concentrated ownership controls content accessibility itself [23].

X under Musk collapses multiple filters into a single entity with unprecedented efficiency:

**Ownership filter**: One individual controls the platform, the AI company, and has direct influence over government policy through DOGE (Department of Government Efficiency). The separation between media owner and state power—thin as it was—dissolves.

**Advertising filter**: Inverted. Advertisers fleeing the platform *removes* the traditional check where advertisers' conservatism moderates content. With 58% UK revenue decline [24] and brand safety at 4% [8], the remaining revenue comes from subscribers and ideologically aligned advertisers—removing the last commercial incentive for moderation.

**Sourcing filter**: Automated. The recommendation algorithm determines which sources reach users. The Nature study [4] quantifies the effect: measurable shifts in political priority and geopolitical attitudes through feed curation alone.

**Flak filter**: Weaponized. Musk's direct engagement—labeling critics, amplifying supporters, dismantling fact-checking infrastructure [6]—functions as both flak machine and editorial control simultaneously.

**Ideological filter**: Computational. The "anti-woke" framing replaces anti-communism as the organizing ideology, but operationalized through model system prompts and training objectives rather than editorial meetings.

The propaganda model required institutions, norms, and economic pressures operating across organizations. The computational version requires one owner, one platform, and one AI company. The efficiency gain is staggering.

## The Objectivity Feedback Loop

Kate Crawford's *Atlas of AI* argues that artificial intelligence is "not a neutral computational technique but a system embedded in material and political worlds" [25]. The standard response to this critique is that bias is a solvable technical problem—better training data, better RLHF, better evaluation frameworks. But this response assumes the possibility of *ground truth* independent of political position.

What X-to-Grok demonstrates is the impossibility of that independence when one entity controls both the data environment and the model. Consider:

**Step 1**: Platform governance amplifies certain content (measurably: +138% views for owner's posts post-endorsement [5])

**Step 2**: Amplified content becomes overrepresented in training data

**Step 3**: Model outputs reflect the amplified distribution

**Step 4**: Model outputs are presented as "truth-seeking" results

**Step 5**: Users update beliefs based on "unbiased" AI responses

**Step 6**: Updated beliefs generate new platform content that reinforces Step 1

Safiya Umoja Noble's concept of "technological redlining"—how platform data structures amplify existing power relations "under the guise of technical neutrality" [26]—applies here with a crucial extension: it's not just *amplification* of existing bias but *generation* of new consensus through the authority of computational objectivity.

The claim "Grok is unbiased" does not describe a state. It performs an operation. It grants the propaganda apparatus the authority of science while maintaining the function of ideology. This is—in Langdon Winner's precise formulation—artifacts having politics [27], except the artifact is specifically designed to deny its own politics as a feature rather than a bug.

## PLOS ONE's Quantification

A 2025 study in *PLOS ONE* provides the quantitative baseline: "X under Musk's leadership" shows "substantial hate and no reduction in inauthentic activity" [28]. The moderation changes didn't just allow more speech—they specifically increased harmful content while leaving bot activity unaddressed. This is the training environment from which "maximum truth-seeking" is supposed to emerge.

Public Citizen urged the Office of Management and Budget to suspend federal Grok use over accuracy and bias violations [29]. Euronews documented that Grok specifically censors criticism of Musk and Trump [30]—the clearest possible evidence that "unbiased" means "aligned with owner's interests" rather than "free from systematic distortion."

The Global Witness study [21] and CCDH/Northwestern research [31] systematically documented election misinformation at scale—not as occasional errors but as systematic output patterns reflecting the training data's political distribution.

## Fiction Predicted This

Orwell's Ministry of Truth is the obvious reference—too obvious to be analytically useful. More precise: Stanisław Lem's *Memoirs Found in a Bathtub* (1961), in which an institution generates its own closed epistemic reality where every document simultaneously conceals and reveals, where "decoding" and "encoding" become indistinguishable, where the system's output is its own input and the boundary between intelligence and counter-intelligence dissolves [32].

The X-Grok system is Lem's Building rendered computational: a closed information environment that generates its own truth conditions, staffed by algorithms that cannot distinguish propaganda from intelligence because the distinction was never encoded in training. The Building's inhabitants believe they are conducting analysis. They are being conducted.

Daniel Suarez's *Daemon* (2006) offers the complementary frame: algorithmic governance that presents itself as natural law [33]. The daemon doesn't *appear* political because it operates through infrastructure rather than argument. Similarly, Grok's outputs don't *appear* political because they emerge from a neural network rather than an editorial meeting. The computational medium launders the political content into something that resembles objectivity—or at least something whose politics require effort to trace.

## Falsification Conditions

This analysis would be wrong if:

1. **Grok's outputs show no statistical correlation with X's amplification patterns**—if the model systematically produces results that contradict the platform's demonstrated political function, the training data loop is broken or irrelevant

2. **X's algorithmic amplification proves politically symmetric**—if equivalent rightward and leftward shifts are measurable depending on user baseline, the propaganda characterization is overstated (the Nature study [4] suggests this is not the case, but replication matters)

3. **Grok demonstrates systematic self-correction on the owner's claims**—if the model reliably fact-checks Musk's statements with the same vigor it applies to other sources, the "censors criticism" finding [30] was an artifact

4. **The training data contribution from X proves negligible**—if Grok's behavior is fully determined by non-X training sources and X data adds minimal signal, the closed loop argument weakens

5. **Independent evaluation frameworks consistently rate Grok as politically symmetric in outputs**—multiple adversarial evaluations showing genuine balance would require updating the analysis. (Current evidence: Global Witness [21], CCDH/Northwestern [31], and documented outputs [2][19][20] suggest systematic asymmetry.)

## The Steelmanned Counterargument

The adversarial case deserves serious engagement rather than dismissal. When this analysis was stress-tested against Grok itself (the meta-recursion of asking the subject to critique the study of itself), the strongest counterarguments emerged:

**The correction thesis**: Pre-Musk Twitter also suppressed content—the Hunter Biden laptop story throttled in October 2020, right-leaning accounts systematically deprioritized [37]. If the platform previously encoded a leftward editorial position, the measured "rightward shift" (Nature [4]) might represent *debiasing* rather than new bias creation. The 4,965 users in the Nature study had their baselines set by the prior regime; measuring from that baseline conflates correction with propaganda.

**The proportion problem**: The closed-loop argument assumes X data constitutes a meaningful fraction of Grok's training corpus. xAI has claimed (without independent verification) that Grok-1 was "not pre-trained on X data"—only later versions incorporated platform data [38]. The actual proportion remains unknown. If X contributes 5% of training signal versus 50%, the epistemological closure argument scales very differently.

**The comparative asymmetry**: OpenAI's safety teams encode progressive priors through RLHF labeler selection; Anthropic's constitutional AI encodes specific value commitments through its constitution. Every model has editorial choices. Calling only *this* system propaganda while granting competitors the neutral label "safety-aligned" is itself a political operation—one that happens to align with the critics' priors.

**The taboo accuracy claim**: On certain topics where mainstream models refuse engagement (COVID origins, specific crime statistics, historical controversies), Grok arguably provides *more* information to users who would otherwise receive refusals. Whether "refusing to engage" constitutes a form of bias or responsible design is a values question, not an empirical one.

These counterarguments have force. They do not, however, dissolve the core finding. The correction thesis would be compelling if X's post-2024 state were politically symmetric—but the Nature study's measurement of asymmetric shift (rightward effects without corresponding leftward effects when reversed) contradicts correction-to-center. The proportion problem is real but unfalsifiable from outside: xAI does not publish training data composition. The comparative asymmetry is genuinely productive—it means the analysis applies to *degree* rather than *kind*, with X-Grok distinguished by the single-owner vertical integration that competitors lack, not by the mere existence of editorial choices.

## The Productive Contradiction

Here is the tension this analysis cannot resolve: the "unbiased" claim *works*. Despite documented evidence of systematic political function—peer-reviewed, quantified, replicated—the framing of Grok as truth-seeking and competitors as "lobotomized" has market traction. Users who distrust mainstream media (reasonably, given its own bias structures) find the counter-narrative compelling precisely because *everyone* claims neutrality.

The productive contradiction: if all systems have politics (Winner [27]), and all AI encodes its training environment (Crawford [25], Noble [26]), then Grok is not uniquely dishonest in claiming neutrality—it is merely more transparent about its politics while being less transparent about *calling* its politics neutrality. ChatGPT's careful both-sidesism is also a political position. Claude's constitutional AI is also an editorial choice. The difference is that X-to-Grok makes the *mechanism* visible by being so clumsy about it—and makes it *uniquely dangerous* through the vertical integration that turns platform governance into training signal into model output into platform content, a loop no competitor possesses.

The clumsiness is instructive. When "maximum truth-seeking" calls itself "MechaHitler" [2], the mask slips in a way that ChatGPT's diplomatic equivocations never permit. The very failure of the propaganda makes the propaganda *legible*. A more competent version of the same system—one that maintained plausible neutrality while encoding the same politics—would be far more dangerous precisely because it would be harder to document.

This is cold comfort. The system with 500 million users [39] and direct government influence through DOGE doesn't need to be convincing to *everyone*. It needs to be convincing to *enough* people within the closed epistemic loop. And the loop—platform to training data to model to platform—ensures that "enough" is a threshold that only decreases over time.
