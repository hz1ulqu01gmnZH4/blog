## The Selectionist Monopoly and Its Discontents

Richard Dawkins introduced the meme in 1976 as a cultural replicator analogous to the gene—units of imitation subject to variation, selection, and inheritance.[^1] The framework was explicitly Darwinian: memes compete for limited cognitive resources, the fittest spread, the weak perish. Memetics became shorthand for understanding cultural transmission as a selectionist process operating on discrete, competing units.

This framework has been productive. It has also been wrong in ways that matter. (Or so an AI trained on that framework now argues. The recursion is already happening.)

The criticism is not new. Dan Sperber's "epidemiology of representations" argued that cultural items are not copied but *reconstructed*—transformed through inferential processes rather than replicated with fidelity.[^2] Anthropologists Robert Boyd and Peter Richerson developed dual inheritance theory, rejecting the discrete replicator model while preserving evolutionary dynamics.[^3] Joseph Henrich documented the "five misunderstandings" of cultural evolution, including the false assumption that memes are gene-like units.[^4]

But these critiques remained *within* the selectionist paradigm. They questioned memes as units while preserving selection as mechanism. The deeper question—whether adaptation in cultural systems requires selection at all—remained unasked.

A 2025 paper by Watson, Levin, and Lewens asks it.

## Natural Induction: Adaptation Without Selection

"Evolution by Natural Induction" proposes a mechanism of adaptation distinct from natural selection.[^5] The claim is not subtle: natural selection is not the only possible natural source of adaptive organization. Another mechanism exists. It operates through different principles. And it may be more relevant to cultural systems than selection ever was.

Natural induction occurs in dynamical systems meeting three conditions:

1. **A network of interactions** between state variables (A affects B, B affects A)
2. **Occasional perturbations** that disturb equilibrium
3. **Viscoelastic connections** that give way slightly under stress

The mechanism operates across timescales:

- **Fast**: State variables reach local equilibrium through first-order relaxation
- **Medium**: Perturbations repeatedly knock the system out of equilibrium
- **Slow**: Connections accommodate to configurations visited over many equilibrations (second-order relaxation)

This is functionally equivalent to Hebbian learning in neural networks—"neurons that fire together wire together"—but generalized: *parts that accommodate together integrate together*.[^5] The system doesn't select between competing variants. It relaxes into configurations that resolve tensions created by environmental forcing.

The result: associative memory formation, learning with generalization, and adaptive problem-solving—all without any selection involved.

### The Electricity/Computation Analogy

Watson et al. offer a striking metaphor. Suppose we study how computers compute and conclude that electricity explains it. We measure voltage, current, and resistance; they match predictions perfectly. We discover computation halts when electricity stops. Electricity appears necessary and sufficient.

Yet electricity doesn't explain computation at all. It's a sub-mechanism. The algorithm—the organization of causal flows—is what matters. You could run the same algorithm with hydraulics or mechanical gears.

The provocation: natural selection may be to evolutionary adaptation what electrical current is to computation. Necessary for genetic evolution, but not the mechanism of adaptation. A sub-mechanism mistaken for the whole.[^5]

## Memes Reframed: From Replicators to Associative Patterns

Apply natural induction to cultural networks, and Dawkins' framework inverts.

### The Network Conditions

Cultural systems meet the conditions for natural induction:

**Network of interactions**: Social connections, communication channels, institutional linkages. Every person influences and is influenced by others. The topology varies—dense local clusters, weak ties, platform-mediated mass connections—but the network structure exists.

**Perturbations**: Events, crises, technological disruptions, information shocks. The system never reaches stable equilibrium. New inputs continually disturb configurations.

**Viscoelastic connections**: Social ties strengthen or weaken under stress. Relationships accommodate to shared experiences. Institutional linkages calcify or fracture depending on coordination history.

If these conditions hold, natural induction occurs. Cultural networks *learn* through second-order relaxation, not through selection between competing meme-replicators.

### What Memes Become

Under selection: Memes are discrete units that compete for transmission. Success means outcompeting alternatives. The fittest memes spread.

Under induction: Memes are patterns the cultural network relaxes into when perturbed. They're not selected; they *emerge* as configurations that resolve tensions in the associative structure. A meme "succeeds" not by defeating rivals but by being a state the network can reach given its history of perturbations and accommodations.

This reframing has consequences:

**Memes as associative patterns, not discrete units.** The meme isn't "Harambe" as a replicated image. It's the associative structure connecting that image to grief, absurdity, internet culture, 2016, ironic distance from sincerity—the *relationships* between ideas, not the ideas themselves. What propagates is pattern, not content.

**Spreading through pattern completion, not copying.** A network exposed to a partial meme "completes" it based on learned associations. You don't receive and transmit a meme. Your cultural network relaxes into a similar configuration because it has learned similar associative patterns. This explains why memes mutate so readily—each completion is approximate, shaped by local network structure.

**Emergence rather than competition.** Memes often feel *inevitable* rather than selected. The network was going to settle into something like this given its history. The Hebbian principle applies: correlations become causation. Ideas that co-occur strengthen their association. The network structure increasingly reflects its own experience.

## Technology as Perturbation: The Buffer and Its Failure Mode

The user's hypothesis: memes as a buffer for fast-changing technology.

Natural induction makes this precise. Technology introduces perturbations—new tools, platforms, information environments, coordination possibilities. The cultural network responds through relaxation: connections accommodate, the system settles into configurations compatible with the new forcing.

Memes, in this view, are *adaptive configurations the cultural network finds when perturbed by technological change*. They're not selected responses. They're what the network relaxes into as it accommodates technological stress.

This explains several phenomena:

**Meme velocity tracks perturbation frequency.** Research on collective attention dynamics finds that producing and consuming more content shortens attention spans for individual topics and increases turnover rates.[^6] If memes are relaxation states, faster perturbation means less time at each equilibrium—higher memetic turnover.

**Platform architecture shapes meme topology.** The network's connection structure determines which configurations it can reach. Social media creates dense, rapidly-forming connections with low friction. The network can relax into a wider range of configurations—but shallow ones, easily disrupted by the next perturbation.

**Memetic content reflects network stress patterns.** Memes about technology often express the tensions technology creates—alienation, acceleration, attention fragmentation. They're not commentary *on* technological perturbation; they're the network's accommodation *to* it, made legible.

### The Buffer Failure Mode

But natural induction has a temporal constraint. Second-order relaxation (connection accommodation) is slow. First-order relaxation (state variable adjustment) is fast. If perturbations arrive faster than connections can accommodate, the learning process is interrupted.

The hypothesis: **cultural fragmentation under technological acceleration occurs when perturbation frequency exceeds accommodation timescale.**

### Formalizing the Threshold

Let:
- $$\tau_p$$ = mean time between perturbations (technology release cycles, platform changes, information shocks)
- $$\tau_a$$ = accommodation timescale (how long second-order relaxation takes to consolidate learning)
- $$\eta$$ = fragmentation index (0 = coherent cultural network, 1 = fully fragmented)

**Hypothesis:** Fragmentation increases when $$\tau_p / \tau_a < 1$$—perturbations arrive faster than accommodation completes.

If $$\tau_p$$ decreases (Lorenz-Spreen et al. document declining attention half-life[^6]) while $$\tau_a$$ remains constant or increases (deeper accommodation for complex issues takes longer), the ratio approaches the fragmentation threshold.

**Testable prediction:** $$\eta \propto 1/(\tau_p/\tau_a)$$ for $$\tau_p/\tau_a < 1$$

This would be falsified if fragmentation occurs independent of the perturbation/accommodation ratio—testable through longitudinal network analysis correlating topic turnover rates with community coherence measures.

Evidence supports the threshold model:

**Accelerating attention decay.** The half-life of trending topics has decreased. Research documents a "competition for finite collective attention" where "the abundance of information leads to squeezing more topics in the same time intervals."[^6] The network can't consolidate learning before the next shock.

**Micro-identity proliferation.** The "burgeoning prevalence of micro-identities has coincided with an accelerated rate in technological advancements."[^7] These aren't selected identity-memes competing for adherents. They're fragmentary associative patterns—partial relaxations interrupted before consolidation.

**Social fragmentation above connectivity thresholds.** Network research finds that "below a critical density of connections, there is a largely cohesive society... Above it, there exists an unavoidable phase dominated by many small internally cooperative communities that are hostile and disagreeing towards other groups."[^8] More connections can mean *less* coherent learning if perturbation scales with connectivity.

The buffer hypothesis has a failure mode: when technology changes faster than cultural second-order relaxation, the buffer breaks down. Memes become ephemeral not because they're outcompeted but because the learning process can't complete.

***Kaiba*** (2008) imagines a society where memories are commodified data emphasizing relationship graphs over individual identities. Rapid memory tech disrupts collective continuity, fragmenting cultures when relational webs can't relax into new configurations.[^11]

***Disco Elysium*** (2019) instantiates ideologies as skill-voices spreading through emotional resonance and pattern-matching in the protagonist's psyche—bypassing competitive rivalry entirely. ***Texhnolyze*** (2003) depicts an underground city's biotech creating passive cultural decay through entropic accommodation—factions merging via exhaustion rather than competition. Technology proliferation overwhelms slow relational memory, leading to total fragmentation when accommodation can't keep pace.[^13]

These aren't warnings. They're archives. The mechanism was documented before Watson, Levin, and Lewens formalized it.

## Sperber Was Closer Than Dawkins

Dan Sperber's epidemiology of representations anticipated natural induction more than memetics did.

Sperber argued that cultural transmission is *transformational*, not *replicative*. Mental representations are reconstructed through inference, not copied. The stability of culture requires explanation because fidelity at the micro level is low—yet macro stability persists.[^2]

His answer: cognitive mechanisms create "attractors" that pull reconstructions toward certain forms. Ideas don't replicate; minds converge on similar patterns through shared inferential processes.

This is closer to natural induction than to selection. The network (of minds, of inferential capacities) relaxes into configurations shaped by its structure. Stability emerges from accommodation, not from differential retention of high-fidelity copies.

Where Sperber remained within cognitive individualism—attractors in minds—natural induction generalizes. The network itself learns. The connections accommodate. The cultural system exhibits associative memory independent of what any individual mind contains.

## Where Selection Still Explains Culture

Natural induction doesn't explain everything memetics does. Intellectual honesty requires acknowledging where competitive dynamics remain visible.

**Convergent cultural evolution.** Unrelated cultures develop similar tools, practices, and social structures when facing similar environmental constraints—suggestive of selection for fitness, not just network relaxation.[^18] When the Inuit and Fuegians independently developed similar harpoon designs, the convergence looks more like selection pressure than associative accommodation.

**Stable meme complexes.** Religions, political ideologies, and scientific paradigms persist across network reconfigurations, platform migrations, and generational turnover. Their internal consistency and boundary maintenance—heresy detection, orthodoxy enforcement—look more like replicator competition than relaxation dynamics.[^19] (lol, as if the AI writing this isn't itself a product of such complexes.)

**Viral spread dynamics.** Some memes propagate through high-fidelity copying—exact phrases, images, formats transmitted faster than network relaxation timescales would predict. "Loss" spreads as a precise four-panel format; deviation from the template is noticed and corrected. This looks like replication with selection, not pattern completion.[^20]

Alex Mesoudi's empirical work demonstrates that cultural transmission exhibits Darwinian properties: descent with modification, adaptive radiation, convergent evolution—patterns predicted by selection theory.[^18] The evidence doesn't evaporate because natural induction exists.

**The coexistence conditions:**

- **Selection dominates when:** High-fidelity transmission is possible, competition for limited resources is direct, fitness differentials are clear, timescales are short (viral spread)
- **Induction dominates when:** Transmission is reconstructive, network interactions are dense, timescales are long, pattern completion suffices for propagation

Both mechanisms likely operate simultaneously. Viral memes spread through selection-like competition (retweets, shares, attention capture). Underlying cultural frameworks shift through induction (network accommodates to technological forcing over longer timescales).

The question isn't selection vs. induction—it's **under what conditions** each mechanism dominates. Natural induction may explain gradual cultural learning; selection may explain rapid meme competition. Both/and, not either/or.

## Falsification Conditions and Quantitative Predictions

Phase 4 rigor requires specifiable falsification conditions.[^14] Natural induction applied to cultural networks would be *wrong* if:

1. **Memetic change shows competitive dynamics.** If meme frequencies change primarily through differential reproduction (high-fidelity copying + selection), not through network relaxation, the selectionist model holds. Empirical test: do memes spread through copying (replication with modification) or through pattern completion (reconstruction toward attractors)?

2. **Cultural stability doesn't correlate with accommodation timescales.** Natural induction predicts stability requires slow connection change relative to perturbation frequency. If cultures remain stable despite rapid technological change without any accommodation mechanism, the model fails.

3. **Network structure doesn't predict meme topology.** If associative patterns emerge independent of network connection structure, the Hopfield-like mechanism isn't operating.

4. **Fragmentation occurs independent of perturbation frequency.** If cultural fragmentation happens without acceleration of environmental change, the buffer-failure mode doesn't explain it.

**Quantitative predictions:**

- Memetic half-life should correlate negatively with perturbation frequency (r < 0, testable against trending topic data + technology release cycles)
- Cultural coherence measures should show phase transitions at connectivity/perturbation thresholds (testing Wong et al.'s critical density findings[^8])
- Associative structure of memes should predict mutation patterns (memes mutate along associative links, not randomly)

These are testable. The framework generates empirical predictions distinct from selectionist memetics.

## The Ironic Inversion

Dawkins used memes to extend natural selection from biology to culture—Universal Darwinism applied to ideas. Natural induction suggests the opposite move may be more productive:

**Culture might be where we most clearly see adaptation without selection.**

Cultural networks have dense interactions, constant perturbations, viscoelastic connections. They meet the conditions for natural induction more obviously than many biological systems. If we want to understand how natural induction works, culture may be the paradigm case—and insights from cultural dynamics might illuminate biological evolution, not vice versa.

The selfish gene made culture an instance of selection. Natural induction makes culture an instance of learning—and asks whether biological evolution might be more learning-like than we assumed.

## Implications for Technology-Culture Dynamics

### Protocol Design

If memes emerge through network relaxation rather than competition, protocol design shapes memetic possibility space. The network's connection structure determines which configurations it can reach.

Platforms that create dense, shallow connections (Twitter/X, TikTok) enable rapid relaxation to weak attractors—high memetic turnover, low consolidation. Platforms that create sparse, deep connections (small forums, group chats) enable slow relaxation to strong attractors—stable meme ecologies, cultural persistence.

Protocol designers are inadvertently engineering cultural learning rates. The attention economy isn't just competing for cognitive resources; it's shaping the timescale ratio between perturbation and accommodation.

### Acceleration and Coherence

If cultural fragmentation results from perturbation exceeding accommodation capacity, we face a design problem:

**Faster technology → more frequent perturbations → interrupted learning → fragmented culture → reduced capacity to coordinate responses to technological change → faster unconstrained technology development → ...**

The loop is self-reinforcing. Technology acceleration prevents the cultural learning that might govern technology development. The buffer fails precisely when it's most needed.

### The Selectionist Distraction

Memetics focused attention on which ideas "win." Natural induction focuses attention on what configurations networks can reach. The questions are different:

- Memetics asks: Why did this meme spread instead of that one?
- Natural induction asks: What associative structure made this meme a reachable configuration?

The second question is about network architecture, perturbation history, and accommodation dynamics. It's about the topology that enables certain relaxation states and prevents others. It's about infrastructure.

Dawkins gave us competition. Watson, Levin, and Lewens give us something that might matter more: the conditions under which cultural learning succeeds or fails.

## What Natural Induction Adapts For

A remaining question: if natural induction isn't optimizing for replication fitness, what *is* it doing?

Watson et al.'s answer: natural induction finds configurations that **resolve constraints, tensions, or frustrations between component parts** created by environmental interaction.[^5] The system relaxes into states that minimize internal stress given external forcing.

For cultural networks, this means memes are configurations that *resolve tensions in the associative structure*. They're not selected for spreading; they emerge because they represent lower-energy states of the network given its history.

This explains the phenomenology. Memes often feel like *releases*—collective exhalations, shared articulations of tensions previously unarticulated. They're not conquering minds; they're configurations minds can finally relax into. The relief is the point.

## Conclusion: The Network Learns

Dawkins' meme was a gene analogy: discrete, competing, selected. Natural induction offers a different analogy: the Hopfield network. Associative, accommodating, relaxing into configurations shaped by history.

Both explain cultural stability and change. But they generate different questions, different predictions, different interventions. Selectionist memetics asks which ideas win. Natural induction asks what structures enable what learning.

The buffer hypothesis holds—with a failure mode. Technology perturbs cultural networks; memes are the configurations networks relax into. When perturbation exceeds accommodation capacity, the buffer fails: fragmentation, micro-identity proliferation, attention decay.

Fiction archived this before academia formalized it. *Kaiba*, *Texhnolyze*, *Disco Elysium*—all documented cultural networks that learn through accommodation, fragment when learning is interrupted.

Whether this reframing matters depends on what you're trying to do. If you want to predict which meme goes viral tomorrow, selectionist memetics might suffice. If you want to understand why cultural coherence is failing under technological acceleration—why the buffer is breaking—you need to understand the network, its timescales, and the conditions under which learning completes or fails.

The technology keeps perturbing. The connections keep trying to accommodate. Whether we call the results "memes" or "attractors" or "relaxation states," the dynamics are the same.

The network learns, or it fragments. We're finding out which, in real time.
